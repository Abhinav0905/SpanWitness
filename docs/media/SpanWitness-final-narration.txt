Pole inspection photos leave a reviewer with three questions. Is there a visible concern? Has the asset changed? Is the image clear enough to support either conclusion? Camera movement and poor contrast can make those decisions harder.

Span Witness keeps the photos, supported image measurements and next inspection step together for each asset.

Open C V five is the image-processing engine. It checks sharpness, detects edges with Canny and finds long line candidates with a Hough transform. Inside the operator-marked pole and reference regions, Huber line fitting estimates their orientations. The app then compares those image angles.

The same pipeline runs on Amazon Web Services. A P I Gateway sends requests to Lambda. Private S three stores images, and Dynamo D B stores observations, comparisons and review requests.

Now let's follow one inspection in the working application.

This is a real, openly licensed photo of a pole beside a house. The gold and blue marks select the pole and building reference. After analysis, the measured difference is nineteen point three degrees in this photograph. It is not a measurement of physical pole lean.

The app proposes a structural alignment inspection. A reviewer can inspect the original and annotated images, add a note and confirm a provisional request. The saved record keeps the evidence attached. No external crew notification is sent.

A second real photograph demonstrates the other outcome. Here, the marked pole edge lacks enough support. The app returns no angle and requests a clearer observation. These are different assets, not a before-and-after pair.

For dated observations of the same asset, history compares a baseline with a later inspection. Open C V uses O R B features and RANSAC to check the shared background. Reference and perspective checks can reject unsuitable comparisons. Repeatable capture and a fixed reference are still required. The deployment view shows the actual hosted address and Open C V runtime. In AWS, we read back the same real-photo observation from Dynamo D B and verify the stored image hash in S three.

Sixty-two software tests and one hundred and eight controlled history checks passed. The two real photos are selected demonstrations, not a field accuracy benchmark.

This prototype does not diagnose cracks, predict a pole falling or calculate fire probability. Its purpose is to help an inspector decide what to review next, with the evidence and its limits attached.
